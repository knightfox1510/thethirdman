# Jasprit Bumrah: why he is unplayable (research brief + storyboard plan, 19 Nov 2026)
Working title: **Jasprit Bumrah: Why He Is Unplayable | Data Story Ep 16** (long, about 9-10 min) + a vertical Instagram/Short of about 160 s. Look: new "blueprint" (navy, cyan line-art, amber), see out/bumrah_storyboard_*.jpg. Nothing is voiced or rendered.
Confidence tags: **OURS** = our Cricsheet/Sportmonks analysis (said "in our archive" on screen) · **2 SRC** = two outlets agree · **1 SRC** = one outlet only (attributed or dropped) · **GENERAL** = published research on fast bowlers, not about Bumrah.

## 1. The thesis
He should not work: a stuttering short run-up, a bent-the-wrong-way arm, a back that has fractured. Yet he has the best Test bowling average of any bowler with 200 wickets. The video explains why (action, release, backspin), shows it in data (stumps, old ball, any pitch), then pays the bill (the back, the workload) and asks the open question: is the trade worth it, and is he the best ever?

## 2. What we found
**The numbers**
- 52 Tests, 234 wickets, average 19.79, strike rate 42.9, economy 2.77 (OURS; agrees with cricket.com.au: 52 Tests, 234 wkts, 19.79, 2.77). First bowler to 200 Test wickets at an average under 20 (Wisden, ESPNcricinfo: 19.5; ICC list 19.42: Marshall 20.94, Garner 20.97, Ambrose 20.99) **2 SRC**.
- The 47 other pace bowlers with 50+ Test wickets since 2018: average 26.1, strike rate 49.7 (OURS).
- SENA away: 34 Tests, 159 wkts, average 21.5, SR 46.4 (OURS; Sportskeeda: 159 wkts in 34 SENA Tests, 21.46, 46.4) **2 SRC**. At home: 16 Tests, 62 wkts, average 17.7 (OURS). 69% of his Tests were away (Wisden: 73.9% to July 2025).
- 25 of his 52 Tests brought five or more wickets in the match (OURS). Border-Gavaskar 2024-25: 32 wickets at 13.06 (most by an Indian in an away series; one Wisden table shows 12.81: use 13.06 and check). T20 World Cup 2024: 15 wickets at 8.27, Player of the Tournament (Wisden) **1 SRC**.

**How his action works** (reported analysis, mostly media and coaching: say so)
- Short, stuttering run-up. Michael Holding: that limited run-up and strong action put batters off, and a longer run-up with the same action would make him less effective (Sportskeeda; page blocked to us, so quote only as reported) **1 SRC**.
- Elbow hyperextension: Ian Pont (arm straight from wrist to elbow; the forward bend is a hyperextension, allowed for hypermobile joints) and Dr Kumar Satish Ravi (ligament laxity, shallow trochlear notch; adds leverage and speed, within legal limits). ICC allows 15 degrees of straightening (ThePrint, 6 Jan 2025) **2 SRC (both in one article)**.
- Release point: about 48 cm closer to the batter than Kemar Roach's (cricket.com analysis; Channel Seven measured 34 cm in front of the crease; "about 50 cm closer than other top bowlers") **conflicting figures: show "about half a metre", attribute**. Release height about 1.96 m, virtually the same for any length from 2 to 8 m (ESPNcricinfo) **1 SRC**. Our arithmetic: 48 cm at 140 km/h is about 12 ms less reaction time.
- Backspin from a whippy wrist: about 5 cm more bounce than average off a good length; batters call it a "heavy ball" (CricViz / ESPNcricinfo, via search summary) **1 SRC**.
- Stability: coach Arun Singh says he is very stable at delivery, relaxed until release (ThePrint) **1 SRC**. Wasim Akram: Bumrah's yorker is the best in the world, and the new-ball control (ICC, WION) **2 SRC**.

**How he gets wickets (OURS, Cricsheet Tests since 2018)**
- Bowled 31% + lbw 21% = **51.7%** of his wickets, against **32.9%** for the other 47 quicks. Caught 47%.
- **Better with the old ball**: average 23.9 in the first 20 overs (64 wkts), 18.2 after (170 wkts). We do not know why: say so.
- By pitch (match average of all bowlers as a proxy): seaming, 29 Tests, his 16.9 vs 22.6; balanced, 21.0 vs 30.5; flat, 7 Tests, 34.0 vs 47.0. He sits at about 70-75% of the match average on every type of pitch. The answer to "what pitch suits him" is: all of them; green ones make him unplayable, flat ones merely very good.
- IPL (our Sportmonks cache, 2013-2026): death overs economy 7.87 vs 9.60 for the league in the same seasons; powerplay 6.51 vs 7.96; against left-handers 6.35 vs 8.31.

**The cost**
- Back history: 2019 lower-back stress fracture (about four months; no surgery, treated in the UK); 2022 back spasms then a stress fracture, out of the T20 World Cup; **surgery in March 2023** (Agarkar, via ESPN; one report places it in New Zealand); Jan 2025 back spasm in Sydney (no second-innings bowling; out of the Champions Trophy) (Wisden timeline, ESPNcricinfo, myKhel) **2+ SRC**.
- 2025 England: India planned three Tests of five (Agarkar: medical staff advised against consecutive Tests) **2 SRC**. We found **no source** that his pace or method changed after the injuries: say workload changed, not method.
- Action or workload? Singh blames workload and long series, others link the hyperextended, whip-like action to spine stress: show both. General research on fast bowlers: lumbar stress fractures in 24-55% of adult fast bowlers; mixed action, a hyperextended side-bent lumbar spine and shoulder counter-rotation are risk factors; under 3.5 days' recovery tripled injury risk in juniors **GENERAL**. We do not claim Bumrah has a "mixed action" (not established) and do not diagnose.
- Availability (OURS): he played 52 of India's 88 Tests since his debut (Jan 2018); two long absences (7 Tests in 2018-20, 9 Tests Dec 2022 to Dec 2023). India won 42% of Tests with him and 67% without (OURS, to Nov 2025); Wisden (to July 2025): 43.5% vs 70.4%, and its reading: mostly home Tests without him, weaker away opposition, and he is saved for tough tours (73.9% of his Tests away). Counting only tough away Tests, the gap flips. This is the honest "con" and a good surprise.

## 3. Pros and cons (the scene)
Pros: late, close release; same release for every length; backspin bounce and skid; hits the stumps; yorker; new ball and old ball; stable and compact. Cons: back stress history and workload; cannot play every Test; flat pitches lift his average to 34; needs wicket-takers at the other end (Root faced only 21 of 72 balls with Bumrah bowling in the 2025 Test, ESPN: attack the other end); relies on one body.
