# Script playbook: hooks, voice and pacing (from a reference reel, 25 Nov 2026)

Reference: a 135 s vertical (720x1280) from a franchise channel on the same 204-off-53 innings. We take the craft, not the jokes. Never reuse its pun, wording or graphics.

## What it does (use these)
1. **Hook = a universal worry, then a swerve.** First 25 s are wordplay and a joke ("worried about AI taking jobs... imagine being a bowler"), then "So, what exactly did this 15 year old do today?". The hook earns trust with humour before any number.
2. **Inversion beat.** "Dismissed in the 13th over. On paper, not ideal. Except India A needed just 5 more to win." State the bad news, then flip it.
3. **A running conceit, bookended.** The AI/robot gag opens and closes the video ("AI is trained on existing data. This one makes existing data irrelevant."). One idea carries the whole piece.
4. **Deadpan understatement.** "Statistically, this was him taking it easy." "The boundary rope was merely a decorative element." "Talk about conserving energy." Short sentences, dry, no exclamation marks.
5. **Rhetorical tags.** "Ridiculous, right?" "Let that sink in." Each is a breath, placed right after the biggest number.
6. **Our-own derived stats.** 52.8% of balls were sixes ("more likely to be a six than not"); 96.08% of a double century without running; every later fifty took 12 balls. One computed number per beat beats three quoted ones.
7. **Every number is drawn, not just shown.** 53 balls = a grid of dots, 28 pink; 103 vs 52 dots side by side; bars growing; a leaderboard filling slot by slot ("?" placeholder, then the name); a "human verification failed" gag at the end.
8. **Typographic scenes, no burned captions.** Big phrase, small mono label ("// FACT CHECK"), logo in the corner; one idea per frame, a new frame about every 3 s; typewriter and count-up motion.
9. **Calm light palette, one accent colour,** generous white space. It reads as editorial, not as sports TV.

## How it differs from us (keep)
- It is a franchise channel: pride and jokes, one innings, no sourcing. Our edge is analysis and credibility: the verification gate, who got him, small v big teams, technique. Keep the numbers checked; borrow the voice.
- Record claims ("fastest ever", "most sixes in men's professional cricket") need a second source before we say them. Until then: "in our records".

## Checklist for every script
- Line 1 is a hook (a worry, a number, or an inversion) and the subject is named within 15 s.
- One conceit, opened in the first 10 s and closed in the last 10 s.
- One derived stat per data beat; one tag line ("Ridiculous, right?") per video at most twice.
- Every number has a drawn form (grid, bar, slot, ruler) rather than a text card.
- Humour is dry, never at the player's expense (jokes land on the bowlers, the rope, the records, the data).
- No source names, no burned captions (house rules).
